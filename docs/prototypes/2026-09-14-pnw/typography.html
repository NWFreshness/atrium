Flat rooflines, clerestory ribbon windows, an open plan, and a house that reads as part of the landscape: the brief for this region is clean lines over ornament, and heavy timber where you touch it. Type should behave the same way — structure you can see, no flourish, and enough weight to hold up in a downpour of data.

05

Pairings, with a verdict

Four pairings worth shipping, ranked. Two of them are the brief's named combinations; two are the additions I would argue for.

Recommendstructural

The Architect's Office

Outfit 700 / Archivo 400 / Geist Mono

Why: Outfit's single-storey a and squared terminals read as drawn rather than typed — the geometric-sans half of the brief, without Montserrat's width. Archivo underneath is the sturdiest of the body candidates: a grotesque with rectangular counters that stays scannable in a four-column table.

Cost: Outfit is popular in startup land, so it needs the letter-spacing discipline (‑0.015em) and the brass/golden accent to not look like every other dashboard.

Recommendheritage

Timber and Brass

Lora 600 / DM Sans 400 / Geist Mono

Why: the literary-solitude half of the brief. Lora has the calligraphic roots that make a nameplate feel like a firm's rather than a product's, and DM Sans is warmer than Inter without losing its geometry.

Cost: a serif heading over a data product needs restraint — serif KPI figures at $130.0k read as a magazine. Keep Lora for headings and navigation only, never for numerals in a table.

named pairingsafe

The Mailing List

Montserrat 700 / Inter 400 / Geist Mono

Why: both are bulletproof. Montserrat was drawn from Buenos Aires signage and holds a wordmark beautifully; Inter is the most legible option in a dense table at 16px — measured, it is the best of the four for characters-per-line at a fixed measure.

Cost: it is also the default answer, and it reads that way. Inter plus a purple gradient is the visual signature of every AI-generated dashboard; with the PNW palette the pair looks like a well-typeset template, not a firm with a point of view.

display onlycaution

Nameplate

League Spartan 700 + Archivo / Merriweather

Why: League Spartan at 700 is the closest thing here to cast metal signage — it makes "Atrium" feel carved. Merriweather at 900 is the heavyweight editorial option if you want the firm to look institutional.

Cost: measured, League Spartan carries the smallest x-height of all ten faces here (42% against Inter's 55%), so it reads visually smaller at 16px than the body text under it — it needs size compensation and belongs to the nameplate, not the UI. Merriweather's high stroke contrast is the one thing here I could not measure, only judge: at small sizes on a dark ground it looks thinner than it does on white.
